# COO CRO principles

Use these principles when the audit needs business context, qualification logic or funnel judgment.

## Landing-page discipline

Before recommending tactics, resolve as much of the following as evidence allows:
- page role and conversion goal;
- traffic source and intent;
- audience awareness and expected commitment;
- offer and promise;
- proof availability and proof quality;
- CTA and form commitment;
- mobile experience;
- measurement readiness;
- downstream CRM or sales quality.

Do not assume one page length or one CTA model fits every route. Match page depth, proof and friction to intent and awareness.

Audit at minimum:
- acquisition-to-page message match;
- offer clarity;
- above-the-fold comprehension;
- proof relevance and proximity;
- objections and risk;
- CTA clarity;
- form placement and field justification;
- mobile usability;
- tracking readiness;
- CRM/downstream quality when available.

## Funnel discipline

Treat a funnel step as justified only when it materially supports one or more of:
- qualification;
- trust;
- conversion;
- attribution;
- operations;
- compliance.

Do not automatically remove friction. A field, step or eligibility gate may be worth keeping if it improves qualified-lead rate, sales efficiency, compliance or downstream outcome.

Separate channel intent. Examples of distinct contexts include:
- brand search;
- non-brand search;
- mixed/opaque automated traffic;
- cold social;
- retargeting;
- organic;
- email/direct/warm.

## Business truth hierarchy

When available, prefer:
1. revenue / sales outcome;
2. qualified lead / opportunity quality;
3. booked or attended next step;
4. lead conversion;
5. click/engagement proxy.

Do not declare a CRO winner from a weaker proxy when stronger downstream evidence is required and available.
